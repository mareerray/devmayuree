project WC2026-tracker

World Cup dashboard & analytics

A World Cup 2026 tournament dashboard built with React, TypeScript, and Vite. During the tournament, data was retrieved from the football-data.org API and stored in Supabase Postgres. After the tournament, the final data was exported as CSV snapshots, which now power the dashboard’s results, standings, top scorers, team search, and analytics.

Features

  • Home dashboard
    • Full knockout bracket from the Round of 32 to the final, including the third-place match.
    • Top 10 scorer rankings with team crests and names.
    • Six most recent completed matches.
    • Image carousel featuring World Cup 2026 venues and highlights.
  • Analytics
    • Tournament overview with KPI cards for finished matches, in-play goals, goals per match, penalty shootouts, and draws after play.
    • Scoring-by-stage table showing matches, goals, goals per match, penalty shootouts, and draws.
    • Team performance charts for top attacks and best defences.
    • Sortable team statistics table with performance rank, matches, goals, goal difference, and goals per match.
    • Highest-scoring matches ranking based on in-play goals.
  • Tournament pages
    • Group standings for all 12 groups with played, won, drawn, lost, goal difference, and points.
    • Results by matchday and knockout stage.
    • Team search across all 48 teams, including results, next match, scorers, and status.
    • About page with tournament information, fun facts, and external resources.
  • Score handling
    • Penalty-shootout kicks are separated from the in-play score.
    • A 1–1 draw decided 3–4 on penalties is displayed as 1–1 (3–4 pens).
    • Analytics and goal-based rankings use in-play goals only.

Data Architecture

  • During the tournament, match, team, scorer, and standings data was retrieved from the football-data.org API and stored in Supabase Postgres.
  • Supabase SQL views prepared frontend-friendly match data and analytics-ready summaries.
  • After the tournament finished, the data was exported as static CSV snapshots and added to the repository.
  • The React frontend now fetches and parses the CSV files, then joins related records in JavaScript.
  • Match and scorer records store team IDs, while team names, short names, and crest URLs are joined from a separate teams dataset.
  • Analytics data includes a tournament overview, scoring by stage, team performance, and highest-scoring matches.
  • Because the tournament is complete and the data is read-only, the app no longer depends on a live database or external API.

Architecture decision

During the tournament, the app used football-data.org as its live data source and Supabase Postgres to store, clean, and prepare the data through SQL views. After the tournament ended, the final data was exported as CSV snapshots and moved into the repository.

Because the World Cup 2026 tournament is complete and its results are read-only, the app now uses static CSV snapshots instead of querying a live database or external API. This keeps the dashboard fast, simple to deploy, and independent of API rate limits.
